The recommendation

Who should choose nProbe

nProbe is a NetFlow v5/v9/IPFIX probe and collector. Choose it to turn packets from a mirror port or TAP into flows, to collect flows from routers and switches, or to convert between flow formats. It exports to ntopng, to commercial collectors and to Kafka, ElasticSearch, Syslog and databases.

  • You need a probe on a mirror or TAP, or a collector for NetFlow, IPFIX and sFlow.
  • You want Layer-7 visibility (450+ applications with nDPI) in every flow record.
  • You must feed an existing collector such as Cisco, Plixer or SolarWinds, or send flows to ntopng.

The ideal use case

Ideal use case for nProbe

  • Environment: border gateways, switches and routers that export NetFlow, or links up to multi-Gbit. Runs on Linux, FreeBSD, Windows x64, macOS and Raspberry Pi OS.
  • Team: network teams that run a flow collector or ntopng and need accurate accounting.
  • Technical requirements: memory footprint under 2 MB regardless of network size. 1 Gbit with a dual-core CPU and vanilla PF_RING, more with PF_RING ZC.
  • Problem solved: replace low-speed embedded probes, add application data to flows, and upgrade flow versions without touching routers.

Ideal for Every Environment

Deployment scenarios

Flow Probe on Mirror/TAP

This mode can be used to analyse traffic from a mirror or TAP device and export flows in NetFlow v5/v9/IPFIX format or to ntopng.

Mirror (SPAN) ports or TAP devices allow network monitoring tools to observe all packets flowing through the network for for network visibility, troubleshooting, threat detection, and capacity planning, without generating traffic or altering the data path.

  • Mirror Port (SPAN): available on most managed switches, duplicates traffic from selected ports or VLANs to a dedicated monitoring port.
  • TAP Device: transparently copies all network traffic at the physical layer, acting as a bump-in-the-wire and providing a fail-safe method for capturing traffic.

A physical NIC card connected to a mirror can be monitored by nProbe by simply specifying its interface name. This configuration can be used to monitor a mirror port from a switch, or in conjunction with a TAP device by aggregating two directions from two network interfaces.

NetFlow Collector

This mode can be used to collect flows in NetFlow v5/v9/IPFIX format and deliver flows to ntopng.

NetFlow Proxy

In proxy mode it is possible to convert from/to IPFIX/NetFlow v5/v9 in order to smoothly upgrade to newer NetFlow protocol versions while capitalizing on previous protocol versions. So you can for instance convert flows coming from your v5 router into IPFIX and vice-versa.

Inline IPS

In this configuration nProbe acts as a bridge device by applying Layer-7 policies to the bridged traffic.

The tradeoffs

When nProbe is not the right choice

  • nProbe produces and forwards flows. It is not the dashboard: pair it with ntopng or another collector to visualize them.
  • For 100 Gbit links use nProbe Cento instead.
  • It does not store packets: use n2disk when packet-level evidence is needed.
  • Performance figures on this page are per core, from a specific test setup. Size your hardware with the sizing notes.

Performance

nProbe has been designed to keep up with multi-Gbit speeds on commodity hardware. Using a dual core CPU, nProbe can be used for capturing packets at 1 Gbit with no/very little (< 1%) packet loss using vanilla PF_RING (no ZC), or even at higher rate with PF_RING ZC kernel-bypass drivers. FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does nProbe cost?

nProbe Pro costs 299.95 euro, Enterprise S 499.95 euro, Enterprise M 899.95 euro and Enterprise L 1,499.95 euro. A Raspberry Pi embedded edition costs 99.95 euro. Licenses are perpetual and include one year of maintenance and updates.

What is the difference between a probe and a collector?

A probe turns packets from a mirror port or TAP into flows. A collector receives flows exported by routers and switches. nProbe can work in both modes.

Which flow formats does nProbe export?

NetFlow v5, v9 and IPFIX, and it can also deliver flows to ntopng over ZeroMQ.

Do I need ntopng?

No. nProbe can export to any collector. Together with ntopng you get dashboards, alerts and historical analysis.

What if I need 100 Gbit?

Use nProbe Cento, the high-speed probe for 1, 10, 40 and 100 Gbit links.
