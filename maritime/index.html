Network Monitoring for Maritime & Ship Networks

Reliable and controlled Internet connectivity at sea

Providing Internet access on a ferry, cruise vessel, yacht, or offshore platform is fundamentally different from providing connectivity on land.

Available bandwidth can change dramatically as the vessel moves between connectivity systems. Satellite links may have limited capacity or high costs, while cellular, Wi-Fi, or other terrestrial links may be available only in specific areas. At the same time, dozens or thousands of passengers and crew members may be competing for the available bandwidth.

ntop provides the visibility, bandwidth control, and multi-WAN capabilities needed to keep maritime Internet services usable and predictable.

Overview

One Internet connection, many competing users

A vessel’s Internet connection is shared by many different types of users and services.

Passengers may be browsing the web, streaming video, using social media, making video calls, or downloading large files. Crew members may need connectivity for operational applications, administration, communications, and business systems.

Without bandwidth control, a small number of users can consume a disproportionate amount of the available capacity and degrade the experience for everyone else.

nEdge acts as an inline network traffic policer, allowing operators to control how available bandwidth is shared between users and devices.

Bandwidth policies can be applied per user, device, application, or protocol, allowing operators to implement policies such as:

nEdge provides both maximum bandwidth limits and minimum guaranteed bandwidth, together with fair bandwidth sharing and Layer-7 application policies.

  • Guarantee bandwidth for crew and operational systems.
  • Give passengers a fair share of the available bandwidth.
  • Apply different limits to different customer plans.
  • Reserve bandwidth for business-critical applications.
  • Throttle bandwidth-intensive applications.
  • Prevent a small number of users from monopolizing the connection.

Use Cases

Ferries

Ferries can provide passenger Internet access while ensuring that crew and operational systems retain the bandwidth they need.

Multiple connectivity sources can be combined, with policies determining which users and services use each connection.

Cruise ships

Large passenger vessels may have thousands of connected devices. Fair bandwidth allocation and application-aware policies can prevent individual users or applications from degrading service for everyone else.

Yachts

Large yachts increasingly provide Internet access to both passengers and staff. nEdge can manage the available bandwidth and intelligently use multiple connectivity providers, including satellite and cellular connections.

Offshore and commercial vessels

Ships operating far from shore often have expensive or capacity-constrained satellite connectivity. Traffic policies can prioritize operational services and control recreational traffic to keep critical communications available.

Key benefits

  • Multi-WAN routing: Combine satellite, cellular, shore Wi-Fi, and other Internet connections.
  • Automatic failover: Switch to another gateway when connectivity becomes unavailable.
  • Load balancing: Distribute traffic across multiple available connections.
  • Bandwidth policing: Control upload and download bandwidth per user, device, or policy.
  • Fair sharing: Prevent individual passengers or devices from monopolizing the connection.
  • Application-aware policies: Block or throttle bandwidth-intensive Layer-7 applications.
  • Captive portal: Authenticate passengers and associate users with their devices.
  • User-based policies: Apply different connectivity policies to passengers, crew, and staff.
  • Network visibility: Understand exactly how the available bandwidth is being consumed.
  • Operational continuity: Protect critical vessel services from passenger Internet usage.

Overview

Keep everyone connected, even when bandwidth is limited

Maritime Internet connectivity will always be constrained by the links available to the vessel. The challenge is to make the best possible use of that bandwidth.

With nEdge, operators can combine multiple WAN connections, control traffic at the edge, prioritize important users and applications, and automatically adapt to connectivity failures. Combined with ntopng’s traffic visibility, the ntop platform provides the tools needed to operate a reliable and controlled maritime Internet service.
